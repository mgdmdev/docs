## Overview

[Hosted checkout](/guides/subscriptions/quickstart) charges against a plan. **One-off
payments** charge whatever you decide: a ticket priced by the organiser, a cart total
after discounts, a wallet top-up.

Nothing about the amount is derived on our side. You compute it, you send it, we charge
it. That matters when your own server has already applied a promo code and a
[points discount](/guides/order-redemption) — recomputing it anywhere downstream would
be the seam where your total and ours drift apart.

<Note>
  Money is in **minor units** — pesewas for GHS, cents for USD. `46000` is GH¢ 460.00.
  A decimal is refused rather than rounded, because sending cedis where pesewas are
  expected is a 100x error and it should fail loudly.
</Note>

## Before you start

One-off payments are the [Application's](/guides/applications) **Payments capability** —
a separate capability from Subscriptions, because selling a ticket is not selling a
subscription. Enable it in the [developer console](https://console.mgpass.net) and
authenticate with that capability's key in the `X-API-Key` header.

## Mobile money

MoMo is a first-class channel, not a fallback. Most Ghanaian buyers pay that way.

```bash
curl -X POST "https://auth.mgpass.net/v1/payments" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $PAYMENTS_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "reference": "acme-ORD-4821",
    "amount": 46000,
    "customer_email": "ama@example.com",
    "customer_phone": "0244123456",
    "channel": "mobile_money",
    "momo_provider": "mtn"
  }'
```

```json
{
  "payment_id": "pay_01HXR3M4K2",
  "reference": "acme-ORD-4821",
  "status": "pending",
  "amount": 46000,
  "currency": "GHS",
  "channel": "mobile_money",
  "display_text": "Approve the prompt on your phone to complete payment.",
  "replayed": false
}
```

`momo_provider` is `mtn`, `vod` (Telecel) or `atl` (AirtelTigo). It is
optional: send `channel: "mobile_money"` without the wallet details and you get a
payment page restricted to mobile money instead, where the buyer enters their own
number and network.

Show `display_text` to the buyer verbatim. It comes from the payment provider and
describes the prompt they are about to see on their handset — your own wording will
eventually disagree with what actually appears.

The charge is `pending` until the buyer approves. Do not fulfil on this response;
[confirm the payment](#confirming-a-payment) first.

## Cards and other channels

Omit `channel`, or set it to `card`, `bank`, `ussd` or `bank_transfer`, and you get an
`authorization_url` to send the payer to.

```bash
curl -X POST "https://auth.mgpass.net/v1/payments" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $PAYMENTS_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "reference": "acme-ORD-4822",
    "amount": 46000,
    "customer_email": "ama@example.com",
    "callback_url": "https://acme.com/orders/4822/return"
  }'
```

## Guests without an email

A guest checkout often has a phone and no email. For mobile money that is the common
case, not an edge case — so send whichever you hold:

```json
{
  "reference": "acme-ORD-4824",
  "amount": 46000,
  "customer_phone": "024 123 4567"
}
```

The payment provider requires an address on every charge, so when only a phone is given
one is derived from it:

```json
{
  "customer_email": "233241234567@guest.mgpass.net",
  "customer_email_synthetic": true,
  "customer_phone": "+233241234567"
}
```

<Warning>
  **Never send anything to a synthesized address.** It is undeliverable by construction —
  the domain exists to absorb the provider's receipts and nothing else. `customer_email_synthetic`
  is on every payment view precisely so you never have to recognise the domain to know.
</Warning>

Any Ghanaian spelling works and is canonicalised before it is stored or used, so
`024 123 4567`, `+233241234567` and `0241234567` are one customer rather than three. That
matters more than it looks: without it the same person becomes several customers at the
provider, their saved cards stop following them, and per-customer reporting quietly
splits — none of which surfaces as an error.

A number that cannot be read as Ghanaian is rejected with `invalid_phone` rather than
turned into an address. A malformed address is worse than none: it looks real enough that
somebody eventually tries to contact it.

### The guest becomes an account

Phone-only payments are not anonymous, they are just unclaimed.

- If an mgPass account already has that number, the payment is attributed to them at
  charge time — a guest who already has an account does not become a stranger for
  checking out signed out.
- If not, `user_id` stays null until that person proves they own the number by signing in
  with it. Their earlier purchases are then claimed onto their account automatically.

You do not have to do anything for this. Send `user_id` when you know it, the phone when
you do not, and the linking happens underneath.

## Idempotency

`reference` is your own order id and the idempotency key of record — the same contract
as `order_reference` on [order redemption](/guides/order-redemption).

Retry after a timeout and you get the original payment back with `replayed: true`. The
provider is **not** called again, so there is no second charge. Two first-time requests
racing resolve the same way: the loser is handed the winner's payment rather than an
error, because a caller retrying its own timeout should never be told it has a conflict.

References are scoped per Application, so your `ORD-1` and another Application's `ORD-1`
are different payments.

If a response never reached you at all, look it up rather than retrying blind:

```bash
curl "https://auth.mgpass.net/v1/payments/by-reference/acme-ORD-4821" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $PAYMENTS_API_KEY"
```

## If you already take payments directly

If you have an existing integration that charges through Paystack directly and
confirms payments by calling their verify endpoint with your own reference, read this
before you route through us. Its absence has already cost one integrator real money.

When you create a payment here, it is registered with Paystack under **our own
reference**, not yours. Your `reference` travels with it as metadata, but it is not
the identifier Paystack knows the transaction by.

That means your existing verification call breaks the moment you switch over:

```typescript
// BEFORE: verifying directly with Paystack. This worked while Paystack knew
// your reference. Once you route through us, it does not.
const res = await fetch(`https://api.paystack.co/transaction/verify/${orderReference}`, {
  headers: { Authorization: `Bearer ${paystackSecret}` },
});
```

Paystack has never seen your reference, so this is not a mismatch — it is an absence.
You get "transaction not found" for a payment that genuinely succeeded. The buyer has
paid, your verification fails, and nothing is delivered. It is invisible until real
money is in flight, because it only appears at the confirmation step.

Confirm against us instead, using the same reference you sent:

```typescript
// AFTER: confirm against mgPass with your own reference.
const res = await fetch(
  `https://auth.mgpass.net/v1/payments/by-reference/${orderReference}`,
  { headers: { "X-API-Key": paymentsApiKey } }
);
const payment = await res.json();
if (payment.status === "succeeded") {
  // Fulfil.
}
```

Your `reference` is the identifier we key on. See
[Confirming a payment](#confirming-a-payment) for the full pattern, including what to
do when the answer is still `pending`.

<Note>
  The payment view carries `provider_reference` — our reference, the one Paystack
  knows. If you reconcile against the Paystack dashboard or a statement later, match
  on that value, not yours.
</Note>

## Confirming a payment

Ticket issuance, order fulfilment and anything else irreversible hangs off
confirmation, never off the response to `POST /v1/payments`. There are two
confirmation paths, and you should understand the trade-off before you pick one.

### Poll: the path that cannot silently stop

```bash
curl "https://auth.mgpass.net/v1/payments/by-reference/acme-ORD-4821" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $PAYMENTS_API_KEY"
```

Polling [`GET /v1/payments/by-reference/{reference}`](/api-reference/payments/get-payment-by-reference)
with your own reference is the recommended primary path for anything irreversible. It
needs no shared secret, no endpoint registered with us, no signature to verify, and
nothing to misconfigure — there is simply less to go wrong.

That endpoint is also self-healing. If a payment is still `pending` after a short
while, reading it makes us check with the provider before answering, so a confirmation
that never reached us is recovered by the act of asking. You do not opt into this and
do not need to do anything. Poll every few minutes, not every second.

### Webhooks: sooner, but not surer

Webhooks remain available and are still useful as a latency optimisation — they tell
you sooner. Three events, registered on your [webhook endpoint](/guides/webhooks):

| Event | When |
|---|---|
| `payment.succeeded` | Money moved. Fulfil. |
| `payment.failed` | The provider declined it. `failure_reason` says why. |
| `payment.abandoned` | Nobody completed it before `expires_at`. |

`failed` and `abandoned` are separate events on purpose. One buyer was refused, the
other walked away — you will almost certainly word those differently, and you should not
have to re-derive which happened from a status field.

Every event carries the same payload, with `null` where a field cannot apply:

```json
{
  "event_id": "whe_01HXR3M4K2",
  "event_type": "payment.succeeded",
  "timestamp": "2026-09-18T11:24:12.431Z",
  "data": {
    "payment_id": "pay_01HXR3M4K2",
    "application_id": "app_acme",
    "reference": "acme-ORD-4821",
    "status": "succeeded",
    "amount": 46000,
    "currency": "GHS",
    "channel": "mobile_money",
    "provider_reference": "mgp_pay_01HXR3M4K2",
    "failure_reason": null,
    "paid_at": 1789716652,
    "settled_amount": 44000,
    "platform_fee": 2000,
    "split_subaccount": "ACCT_organizer123",
    "user_id": "usr_abc123",
    "metadata": { "order_id": "4821" }
  }
}
```

### Verify the signature

Every delivery is signed HMAC-SHA256 over the raw body with your webhook secret, in the
`X-Mgpass-Signature` header. Verify it before acting — an unverified payment webhook is
an endpoint anyone can use to issue themselves a ticket.

```typescript
import { createHmac, timingSafeEqual } from "node:crypto";

const expected = createHmac("sha256", webhookSecret).update(rawBody).digest("hex");
const received = req.headers["x-mgpass-signature"] as string;

const ok =
  expected.length === received.length &&
  timingSafeEqual(Buffer.from(expected), Buffer.from(received));
if (!ok) return res.status(401).end();
```

Compare in constant time. A plain `===` leaks the signature a byte at a time to anyone
willing to measure.

<Warning>
  Delivery is **at-least-once** and `event_id` is stable across every retry of the same
  event. Dedupe on it, and make fulfilment idempotent — mgPass settles the payment
  exactly once on its side, but it cannot make your ticket issuance safe to repeat.
</Warning>

<Warning>
  **A webhook can fail to arrive, and if it is your only confirmation path a buyer can
  pay and receive nothing.** A real payment has succeeded at the provider with no
  webhook following it — the buyer paid and got nothing until someone asked us
  directly. If you fulfil off webhooks, run a periodic sweep over your own `pending`
  orders against `GET /v1/payments/by-reference/{reference}` as a backstop.
</Warning>

A payment reaches one of these and then stops moving:

| `status` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `pending` | Started, no money confirmed either way. Not a failure — it may still resolve. |
| `succeeded` | Money taken. Fulfil. |
| `failed` | The provider told us it failed. `failure_reason` says why. |
| `abandoned` | The payment window closed with no completion. Different from `failed`: it means no answer ever came, not that anyone declined. |
| `refunded` | Settled and later reversed. |

`abandoned` is a real outcome, not a stuck row. A charge left pending forever would show
up in reconciliation as an unresolved sale, so uncompleted charges are swept once their
window passes. Set `ttl_seconds` to change that window (300 to 86400, default 3600).

<Warning>
  Do not treat a long-lived `pending` as dead. If you void a discount, release stock or
  reverse loyalty points on it, you will do so to buyers whose payment simply had not
  resolved yet. Only `failed` means the money is not coming; only `abandoned` means the
  window closed.
</Warning>

A late failure after a success cannot flip a settled payment back. Once money has moved,
the record reflects that.

## Settlement

When you enable the Payments capability you must say where money goes. There is no
default, because the default would be "our balance" and that is not a thing anyone
should arrive at by omitting a field.

| `settlement_mode` | Use when |
|---|---|
| `destination` | Everything you sell settles to one account. You supply it at enable time and it is created for you. |
| `per_payment` | You sell on behalf of many different payees. You pass a `subaccount` on each charge. |
| `platform` | The money is genuinely yours. Only correct when you are the payee. |

### Payout destinations

A payout destination is a bank account or wallet that a payment's share settles to
directly. Create one per payee:

```bash
curl -X POST "https://auth.mgpass.net/v1/payout-destinations" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $PAYMENTS_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "reference": "organizer-4821",
    "business_name": "Acme Events Ltd",
    "destination_type": "mobile_money",
    "settlement_bank": "MTN",
    "account_number": "0244123456"
  }'
```

`reference` is your own id for the payee — it is how you find the destination again,
and mgPass never learns anything else about who they are. Get valid `settlement_bank`
codes from [`GET /v1/banks`](/api-reference/payments/list-banks).

Then pass the returned `subaccount` when you charge.

By default a payment settles to the platform account.

Pass `subaccount` (or a pre-built `split_code`) and the payee's share settles **directly
to their own account** without passing through the platform balance. `platform_fee` is
the flat amount in minor units you retain.

```json
{
  "reference": "acme-ORD-4823",
  "amount": 46000,
  "customer_email": "ama@example.com",
  "subaccount": "ACCT_organizer123",
  "platform_fee": 2000
}
```

<Warning>
  **`platform_fee` is required whenever you split.** Send `0` to take no fee.

  It is not optional because of how the provider resolves a split: a flat per-transaction
  fee takes precedence over the destination's stored percentage, and the percentage is
  only consulted when the flat fee is missing. That is the one path where the payee's
  share is decided by a value you did not look at on this request, so we refuse it rather
  than guess. An explicit zero is a decision; an absent one is an oversight, and they
  should not look the same when someone else's money is the difference.
</Warning>

A destination also carries `subaccount_percentage` — the share the **payee** receives,
defaulting to **100**. It is a fallback, not the main lever. Prefer the flat fee: it is
stated per transaction, where you can see it.

When the payment settles, `settled_amount` and `platform_fee` record what actually went
where.

This matters beyond convenience. If you are selling on behalf of someone else — an event
organiser, a vendor, a merchant — splitting at the provider means the money owed to them
is never held by anyone else on the way. Their share transfers to them directly, on the
normal payout schedule, and does not sit in the platform balance first.

Splits work on mobile money as well as cards.

<Warning>
  **A payee can be settled to an MTN or Telecel wallet, but not AirtelTigo.** A buyer can
  still *pay* from an AirtelTigo wallet — that restriction is on payouts only. A payee
  whose only mobile money is AirtelTigo needs a bank account instead.

  The list of wallets you can charge and the list you can settle into are different, so
  do not validate both against one enum. Catch it at onboarding: a clear refusal when
  someone signs up beats a held payout after their first event.
</Warning>

### Tell payees this before their first sale

**A new or unverified subaccount has its first payouts held until verification
completes.** Normal scheduling resumes afterwards, but the first one is not immediate.

Whoever onboards a payee owns saying so, at signup, in the flow — not in a support reply
afterwards. A first payout that silently fails to arrive the morning after someone's
launch night is the worst possible moment for that surprise, and by then it reads as
your platform withholding their money rather than a verification step nobody mentioned.
Treat it as part of the onboarding step, not documentation.

**Mobile money settlements are exempt from the minimum payout thresholds** that apply to
bank transfers, which usually makes a wallet the friendlier default for a small payee —
worth defaulting to, rather than leaving them to discover a threshold they never clear.

## Errors

| Status | Code | When |
|--------|------|------|
| 400 | `invalid_request` | `reference` missing, or neither `customer_email` nor `customer_phone` given. |
| 400 | `invalid_phone` | `customer_phone` could not be read as a Ghanaian number. |
| 400 | `invalid_amount` | Not a positive integer in minor units, or above the per-charge ceiling. |
| 400 | `invalid_momo_provider` | Not `mtn`, `vod` or `atl`. |
| 400 | `unsupported_currency` | Not `GHS` or `USD`. |
| 401 | `unauthorized` | Missing or invalid `X-API-Key`. |
| 403 | `channel_not_allowed` | That channel is not enabled for your Application. |
| 429 | `rate_limit_exceeded` | More than 300 charges in the current minute. |
| 502 | `provider_error` | The provider refused. The payment is recorded as failed and `details.payment_id` identifies it. |

Errors use the envelope described in [API conventions](/guides/api-conventions).

## Rate limits

Per Application, per minute: **300** charges, **600** reads.
